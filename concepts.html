Web2 vs Web3

Definition

Web2 is the current internet model built around centralized platforms that own user data. Web3 is the next generation built on decentralized blockchain networks where users own their data and digital assets.

Real-World Example

When you post a photo on Instagram (Web2), Meta owns it and can delete your account at any time. When you mint an NFT (Web3), you own it forever and can sell it on any marketplace.

Advantages of Web3

  • Users own their data and digital assets
  • Censorship-resistant platforms
  • Direct creator-to-consumer monetization
  • Interoperability across platforms

Disadvantages of Web3

  • Steeper learning curve for beginners
  • Gas fees for transactions
  • Less mature user experience
  • Regulatory uncertainty

Ethereum vs Bitcoin

Definition

Bitcoin is digital gold, a store of value and payment network. Ethereum is a global computer that runs smart contracts and decentralized applications (dApps).

Real-World Example

Bitcoin is like owning physical gold that you can send anywhere in the world. Ethereum is like owning a piece of the internet itself, where you can build apps, create tokens, and program financial logic.

Advantages of Ethereum

  • Smart contract programmability
  • Supports NFTs, DeFi, and DAOs
  • Faster block times (12 seconds vs 10 minutes)
  • Massive developer ecosystem

Advantages of Bitcoin

  • Proven store of value over 14+ years
  • Most secure and decentralized network
  • Fixed supply of 21 million coins
  • Simple and robust codebase

Public Key vs Private Key

Definition

A public key is like your email address, you can share it freely so people can send you crypto. A private key is like your email password, it must never be shared because it gives complete control over your funds.

Real-World Example

Imagine a mailbox. The public key is your mailbox address, anyone can drop mail in. The private key is the only key that opens the mailbox. If someone gets your private key, they can take everything inside.

Public Key Advantages

  • Safe to share publicly
  • Used to receive funds and verify identity
  • Can be derived from private key
  • Enables transparent transactions

Private Key Risks

  • Anyone with your private key owns your assets
  • Lost keys mean lost funds forever
  • Must be stored offline or in hardware wallets
  • Phishing attacks target private keys

Blockchain vs Database

Definition

A traditional database is a centralized system controlled by one entity, like a bank's ledger. A blockchain is a distributed database where identical copies exist on thousands of computers, and no single entity controls it.

Real-World Example

A bank's database is like a notebook kept in the bank vault. Only bank employees can write in it, and you must trust them. A blockchain is like a public bulletin board where every copy is identical, and everyone can verify what is written.

Blockchain Advantages

  • No single point of failure
  • Immutable historical records
  • Trustless verification
  • Global accessibility

Database Advantages

  • Much faster transaction processing
  • Lower operational costs
  • Easy to modify and update data
  • Better for private/internal data

Smart Contracts

Definition

Smart contracts are self-executing programs stored on a blockchain that automatically enforce and execute agreements when predefined conditions are met. They are like vending machines, insert money, select item, and the machine delivers automatically.

Real-World Example

Imagine buying a house. Traditionally, you need lawyers, escrow agents, and banks. With a smart contract, when the buyer sends the payment, ownership automatically transfers. No intermediaries, no delays, no disputes.

Advantages

  • No intermediaries or middlemen
  • Execution is automatic and guaranteed
  • Transparent and auditable code
  • Reduces costs and processing time

Disadvantages

  • Code bugs can be catastrophic and irreversible
  • Cannot adapt to unforeseen circumstances
  • Legal recognition varies by jurisdiction
  • Requires technical expertise to write safely

Popular Use Cases

  • DeFi lending and borrowing protocols
  • Automated insurance payouts
  • Decentralized exchanges (DEXs)
  • Supply chain automation
  • Token sales and crowdfunding

Proof of Work vs Proof of Stake

Definition

Proof of Work (PoW) requires miners to solve complex math puzzles using massive computing power to validate transactions. Proof of Stake (PoS) requires validators to lock up (stake) cryptocurrency as collateral to validate transactions.

Real-World Example

PoW is like a lottery where you buy millions of tickets (computing power) to win. PoS is like a savings account where the more money you deposit, the more likely you are to be chosen to validate transactions and earn rewards.

Proof of Work Advantages

  • Proven security over 14+ years (Bitcoin)
  • High barrier to attack (expensive hardware)
  • Decentralized mining distribution

Proof of Stake Advantages

  • 99.95% less energy consumption
  • Faster transaction finality
  • Lower barrier to entry (no expensive hardware)
  • Built-in economic penalties for bad behavior

Layer 1 vs Layer 2

Definition

Layer 1 is the base blockchain (like Ethereum or Bitcoin) that provides the core security and consensus. Layer 2 is a scaling solution built on top of Layer 1 that processes transactions faster and cheaper while inheriting Layer 1 security.

Real-World Example

Layer 1 is like a busy highway with toll booths. Layer 2 is like a service road that runs parallel, where you can travel faster and cheaper, but you still use the main highway for final settlement and security.

Layer 1 Advantages

  • Highest security and decentralization
  • Native asset issuance
  • Foundation for entire ecosystem

Layer 2 Advantages

  • Transaction fees reduced by 90%+
  • Throughput increased by 100x+
  • Same security as Layer 1
  • Better user experience

Gas Fees

Definition

Gas fees are payments users make to compensate for the computational energy required to process and validate transactions on a blockchain. They prevent spam and ensure the network remains secure and functional.

Real-World Example

Think of gas fees like postage stamps. When you send a letter, you pay for the postal service to process and deliver it. Similarly, gas fees pay miners or validators to process your transaction and add it to the blockchain.

Factors Affecting Gas Fees

  • Network congestion: More users = higher fees
  • Transaction complexity: Simple transfers cost less than smart contracts
  • Gas price: Users can bid higher for faster processing
  • Blockchain type: Layer 1 is more expensive than Layer 2

How to Reduce Gas Fees

  • Use Layer 2 networks like Arbitrum or Polygon
  • Transact during off-peak hours (weekends, nights)
  • Use gas price estimation tools
  • Batch multiple transactions when possible

Wallets

Definition

A crypto wallet is software that stores your private keys and allows you to interact with the blockchain. Contrary to the name, wallets do not actually store your coins, they store the keys that prove ownership of coins on the blockchain.

Real-World Example

Your wallet is like a keyring. The keys themselves are not valuable, but they unlock access to your house (funds). If you lose your keys, you cannot access your house. If someone copies your keys, they can enter without your permission.

Hot Wallets (Software)

  • Pros: Free, easy to use, always connected
  • Cons: Vulnerable to hacks, phishing, malware
  • Best for: Small amounts, frequent transactions
  • Examples: MetaMask, Trust Wallet, Coinbase Wallet

Cold Wallets (Hardware)

  • Pros: Maximum security, offline storage, immune to hacks
  • Cons: Cost $50-200, less convenient for frequent use
  • Best for: Large holdings, long-term storage
  • Examples: Ledger, Trezor, Keystone

Consensus Mechanisms

Definition

Consensus is how blockchain networks agree on what transactions are valid without a central authority. It is the mechanism that ensures all participants have the same copy of the ledger and prevents double-spending.

Real-World Example

Imagine a group of friends deciding where to eat. In a centralized system, one person decides. In a consensus system, everyone votes, and the majority wins. The blockchain does this automatically with math instead of human votes.

Types of Consensus

  • Proof of Work (PoW): Solve puzzles with computing power. Used by Bitcoin.
  • Proof of Stake (PoS): Lock up tokens as collateral. Used by Ethereum 2.0.
  • Delegated PoS: Token holders vote for delegates. Used by Cardano.
  • Proof of Authority: Trusted validators only. Used by private chains.

Why Consensus Matters

  • Prevents double-spending without a bank
  • Ensures all nodes agree on transaction history
  • Provides economic incentives for honest behavior
  • Makes the network resilient to attacks
